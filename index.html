Protocol Project
Culture Center 4F
Water Dispenser

Public
Surface,
Private
Object

This project observes how a personal object placed in a public space changes the perceived rule of that surface. A cup-drying rack and one cup were installed above the water dispenser on the 4th floor of the Culture Center.

01 / Assumption

Temporary occupation

People sometimes place personal belongings in public spaces for convenience. Although everyone understands that occupying a shared surface for a long time is not allowed, short-term use often feels acceptable. I assumed that the water dispenser, especially during exam period, could reveal this ambiguous behavior.

keyword: convenience / hesitation / public rule / temporary use / unofficial occupation

02 / Site

Culture Center 4F Water Dispenser

Site condition before intervention

The Culture Center is used continuously during exam period. Students stay for long hours, move between study spaces, and repeatedly use the restroom and water dispenser. Even before the intervention, cups or tumblers were occasionally found on top of the dispenser.

03 / Intervention

Cup-drying rack as an invitation

Cup-drying rack with one personal cup

I placed a cup-drying rack and one cup on top of the dispenser. The rack was intended to act as a subtle invitation: if one object already occupied the surface, would others begin to use the space in a similar way?

04 / Observation Log

Five days of checking

I checked the site two to three times a day. The intervention did not produce the behavior I expected, but the small changes revealed how carefully people read the ownership of objects in public space.

Day 1

No visible change. Only my cup remained on the rack.

Day 2

No visible change. The rack was not used by others.

Day 3

A toothbrush set appeared near the intervention.

Day 4

The toothbrush set disappeared. I moved the rack closer to the front edge.

Day 5

Paper cups appeared in front of the rack, but the rack itself was still unused.

05 / Modification

Making it more visible

The rack was moved closer to the front area

After the toothbrush set disappeared, I questioned whether the rack was too hidden near the wall. To make it more noticeable and easier to access, I pulled it closer to the front of the dispenser. I expected this change to encourage more active use.

06 / Result

Visible, but not usable

The modification increased the visibility of the object, but it did not make people use the rack. Instead, people placed paper cups near the rack without using it directly. This suggests that the surface became slightly more available, while the rack itself remained too private or too specific to touch.

07 / Finding

An object in public space does not automatically become public.

The failure of the prototype revealed a boundary: people may temporarily use the surface, but they hesitate to use an object that appears to belong to someone else.

08 / Reflection

Private object, public hesitation

My initial assumption was that the cup-drying rack would invite others to participate. However, the result showed that convenience alone is not enough to change behavior. The rack may have been interpreted as someone’s personal territory rather than a shared tool.

This failure became the main finding of the project. Public behavior is shaped not only by need, but also by hesitation, ownership, and the unclear status of an object.
