Liquid
Protocol

A small intervention around a water dispenser, exploring how a wet cup, a drying rack, and a public surface can blur the boundary between personal desire and social norms.

Concept

When does a surface become usable?

This project begins from a simple desire: to dry a wet cup near a water dispenser. However, the space in front of a restroom is not officially designed for personal storage. By placing a cup-drying rack in this location, the intervention questions how people read, accept, or resist an unofficial rule created by an object.

refraction / temporary occupation / public behavior / personal convenience / liquid glass

Site

Restroom Water Dispenser

The site is the area around a water dispenser in front of a restroom. It is a functional but ambiguous space: people pass by, drink water, wash cups, and sometimes look for a place to put things down temporarily.

Intervention

Cup Drying Rack

The cup-drying rack acts like a small permission sign without words. Its presence suggests that the space might be used for drying or storing cups, even though no official instruction allows it.

Observation Question

If one wet cup appears, does the rule of the space begin to change?

Reflection

Beyond the Surface

The intervention reveals how social norms are not always fixed by signs or rules. Sometimes, a single object can create a new possibility. The liquid glass style reflects this unstable condition: transparent, distorted, fragile, and constantly shifting depending on how people look at it.
